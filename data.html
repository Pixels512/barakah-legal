How ad funding shows up in prayer-app reviews

An open dataset, with the counting rules published so you can argue with them

We read the public App Store reviews of five widely-used prayer apps — 14,057 reviews across 12 storefronts, pulled 2026-08-13. Most people rate these apps highly. Among the 1,985 reviews rated one or two stars, 867 mention adverts or paywalls.

We publish this because we make a competing app and we would rather be argued with than believed. The raw aggregates are in review-data.json.

Apps are anonymised as A–E: the finding is about how ad funding behaves, not about naming rivals. This is also the most important row of the whole dataset — the pattern is concentrated, not universal.

Method

  • Source. Apple public customer-review RSS feed (itunes.apple.com/{country}/rss/customerreviews)
  • Storefronts. AE, CA, DZ, EG, FR, GB, ID, MA, MY, PK, SA, US. A storefront is a country, not a city.
  • Advertising rule. /\bads\b|\bad\b|advertis|paywall|اعلانات|إعلانات|too many ad/i
  • Population cap. Apple returns recent reviews first and caps at roughly 10 pages per storefront per app. This is a recent-weighted retrievable sample, not a census.

What this dataset does not show

A keyword match is evidence that a review raises a topic — not proof the topic caused the rating. The percentages are of unhappy reviews, not of all users. Apple's feed is recency-weighted and capped, so this is a retrievable sample rather than a representative survey. And the five apps are ones we selected as close competitors: this is not a usage ranking. You can re-do every division here and check each rule against the text we describe; you cannot re-run the count from scratch, because the apps are anonymised and Apple's feed returns different reviews as time passes.
