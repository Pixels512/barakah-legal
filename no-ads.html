A prayer times app with no ads and no tracking

Because worship shouldn't come with an advertisement.

If you are here because an Islamic app you trusted filled up with adverts, or put the Qur'an behind a subscription, you are not imagining it and you are not alone.

What the reviews actually say

We read the public App Store reviews of the five most-used prayer apps — 14,057 reviews across 12 countries. Of the 1,985 reviews rated one or two stars, 902 — about 45% — are about adverts, subscriptions or paywalls. It is by a wide margin the single biggest complaint in the category, and it is the same story in Riyadh, Cairo, Dubai, London and Chicago.

How we counted. Apple publishes a customer-review feed for every app. We pulled it for five prayer apps across 12 storefronts, kept the reviews rated 1–2 stars, and counted how many mention adverts, subscriptions, premium tiers or paywalls, in English or Arabic. The feed only returns recent reviews and caps at roughly 10 pages per storefront, so this is a large sample of current opinion rather than every review ever written. The counting script and the raw reviews are public in our repository, so you can re-run it and check the number yourself rather than take ours.

The honest small print

A few features do reach the internet, and only when you ask for them: searching for a nearby mosque sends your approximate area (rounded to about a kilometre) to an open mapping service; Qur'an audio streams when you play it. None of it comes to us — we run no server — and all of it is listed plainly in our privacy policy. We would rather tell you that than claim a perfect absolute we cannot stand behind.
